# Your AI System Needs A Verification Layer. Here Are 7 Ways To Build One.

With each new model release, we're seeing constant improvements in LLM capabilities, better reasoning, better synthesis. That's according to the benchmarks, anyway. However, a lot of the old problems when building knowledge agents that rely on retrieving information to ground their responses, those problems are just not going away. Hallucinations are still an issue where the model generates outputs that aren't actually backed up by what was retrieved from the data source. And these hallucinations can be obvious to spot manual review, whereas the sneakier one is overextension. This is where the retrieved source that supports, let's say, a narrow claim, whereas the model states it in a much broader, more confident version. There's conflation, where two retrieved chunks get fused into a single claim that neither one of them actually supports. This happens a lot in rag systems, where you essentially have a bag of chunks being returned and mixed together by the model. And then there's citation mismatch. This is where the cited source is real and it was retrieved, but it doesn't actually back up the paragraph that it's attached to. And for knowledge agents that carry out research and analysis, document creation, report generation, these are major problems because every output depends on staying faithful to the retrieved sources at every step in the loop. And as models get bigger, the errors don't necessarily disappear, they just get more subtle and more confidently stated, which actually makes them harder to catch. Even Anthropic's own advice tells users to review Claude's cited sources and to scrutinize any high-stakes advice given by Claude. So, if you're building an AI product, you can't just leave this verification up to chance. You need to actually design for it. In this video, I'm going to show you seven ways to design verification into your agentic system to assist users in verifying the generated outputs and ultimately improve trust levels in the platform. Outside of having a dedicated citation agent, there are two other approaches that you could take. The first is an evidence-first architecture. A variation on that is a claim-first architecture.
