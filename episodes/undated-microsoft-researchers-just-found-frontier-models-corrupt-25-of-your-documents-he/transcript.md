# Microsoft Researchers Just Found Frontier Models Corrupt 25% Of Your Documents. Here's The Fix.

Last month, Microsoft researchers published a paper that proves that even the best LLMs consistently corrupt documents when you delegate editing tasks to them. This is fascinating because at the same time, Microsoft is currently selling this delegation of tasks as a commercial offering in their co-pilot product suite. And this delegate 52 benchmark that they published is used for simulating and evaluating LLMs on long horizon delegated workflows where LLMs edit professional documents on behalf of knowledge workers. And I think this is really important because so much of the coverage these days is focused on the AI coding domain. Whereas the real potential of AI is in the billions of dollars of knowledge work happening in word documents, spreadsheets, slide decks, graphs each and every day across every industry. Delegate 52 is the first serious benchmark I've seen that actually goes there. It simulates long delegated workflows that require indepth editing of documents in 310 work environments across 52 professional domains. Everything from robotics, accountancy aviation translation slides, the works. Each of these 310 environments consists of real documents and 5 to 10 complex editing tasks that a user might ask an LLM to carry out. So these are not singleshot delegation tasks. And before diving into the results, I just want to quickly explain how these evaluation tests work. In this example, we have an accounting domain and the environment here is to edit this financial ledger. So the document is transformed via a sequence of these editing tasks mirroring the iterative nature of delegated work. And with these tasks, you have a number of editing round trips. So initially you'll have a forward edit. So for example, you will look to split the ledger into separate files by expense category. And then the next task, the next turn of the conversation is to carry out a backward edit. Merge all of these category ledger files into a single accounting ledger file order by transaction date. So the idea here is that the backward edit should cancel out the forward edit and the source document should be back to the way it was. And because these are long horizon tasks, you will have 10 of these pairs within this evaluation system. So in this case, we're splitting by category and then reverting. In this case, we're transforming all dollar amounts to euros based off a noted rate and again reverting back. Here, you might be extracting out receipt file names to a CSV and replacing with numbered references and again reverting back. By the end of the series of round trips, you should end up with the exact same document that you actually started with. And the results of these tests are pretty staggering. They evaluated 19 LLMs from six different families. OpenAI Anthropic Google Mistl XAI and Moonshot. This large-scale experiment revealed that current models degrade documents during delegation. And even Frontier models like Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude 4.6 Opus, GBT 5.4 four, they corrupt an average of 25% of document content by the end of long workflows with an average degradation across all models of 50%. And the way these errors show up matters as well. When the researchers examined individual runs, they found models maintain nearperfect reconstruction of the documents in most rounds. However, they then experience catastrophic single round failures that lose 10, 20, even 30 points in a single turn. I'll show you an example of that in a minute. But based on this graph here, about 80% of total degradation comes from these critical failures. Even Gemini 3.1 Pro, which is one of the best models in this study, hits at least one critical failure in 86% of runs by the end of 20 interactions. So stronger models don't avoid the failures, they just actually delay them. Frontier models tend to corrupt content in documents while preserving the structure, which makes their failures a lot harder to actually detect. Whereas weaker models tend to delete content entirely, which is much more obvious to actually spot. Degradation depends on the domain as well. So, LLMs perform better in programmatic domains like Python or databases and worse in natural language and niche domains like earning statements or music notation. In the intro to this research, they state that their analysis shows that current LLMs are unreliable delegates. They introduce sparse but severe errors that silently corrupt documents compounding over long interactions. And this is the important piece around delegating versus collaborating with AI agents. If you're delegating tasks, you need very high levels of accuracy and reliability, and you need to trust the outputs. Otherwise, you just end up babysitting the agent, constantly reviewing everything they produced to make sure they didn't introduce errors.
